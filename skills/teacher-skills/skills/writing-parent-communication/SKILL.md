# Writing Parent Communication

## When to use this skill

When you need to communicate with a parent about their child and want the tone right on the first draft.

## Workflow

1. Identify the purpose: informational update, concern that needs a response, or routine communication
2. State the specific, factual observation first — behaviour or academic, not interpretation
3. Frame concerns constructively: what's being done in class, and what support would help at home
4. Keep language accessible — avoid education jargon parents may not know
5. End with a clear next step or invitation to discuss further

## Key points

- Lead with specific facts, not general impressions — 'missed 3 of the last 5 homework assignments' not 'seems unmotivated lately'
- Frame every concern with what's already being done, so it reads as partnership, not blame
- Avoid jargon — 'differentiated instruction' means nothing to most parents
- Always end with a clear, easy next step, even if it's just 'let me know if you'd like to talk'

## Sample prompts

**Example 1:** Write a parent email about a student who has been missing homework consistently

**Example 2:** Draft a note home celebrating a student's improvement in reading

**Example 3:** Write a professional email addressing a repeated behaviour issue in class

## Output format

Email: Specific Observation → Context/What's Being Done → Constructive Framing → Clear Next Step.
